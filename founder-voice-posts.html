Three founders, three voices, zero templates.

Three LinkedIn posts for three fictional B2B founders. Same job, different people. Each note shows how the voice was built.

By Umar, 3 min read
M
MayaFounder, payroll tool

Our first customer paid us in January.

Our first complaint arrived in January too.

It said: "Why does payroll take 9 clicks?"

I wanted to defend it. Instead I counted. It was 11.

We spent the next month cutting it to 3. Churn dropped. Nobody sent a thank-you note, which is how you know it worked.

If your product needs a tutorial, the tutorial is the bug.

Maya

Direct, dry, short.

One-line paragraphs, a small joke at her own expense, and a closing rule she would actually say out loud.

D
DanielFounder, security startup

Most security reviews ask the wrong question.

They ask "Are we compliant?" The better question is "What would we do at 3 a.m. if this failed?"

Compliance tells you a door has a lock. It doesn't tell you who has the key, or what happens when someone loses it.

Before your next audit, write down the answer to the 3 a.m. question for your three most important systems. If it takes more than a page, that's your real to-do list.

Daniel

Calm, careful, explains first.

Longer sentences, a plain image (door, lock, key) instead of jargon, and a practical step at the end.

P
PriyaFounder, logistics platform

A warehouse manager called me last week. She wasn't angry. She was tired.

"I check four screens before lunch," she said. "Can you give me one?"

I've built software for ten years. That sentence taught me more than any roadmap meeting.

So this quarter we're not adding features. We're removing three screens.

Thank you to the team for being brave enough to delete things we were proud of.

Priya

Warm, story-first.

Opens with a real moment, quotes a customer, and ends by thanking the team, which is how she talks to them.

How I work

I start from the founder's interview notes and an AI draft, then rewrite until the opening, rhythm and opinions belong to the founder. Every post is read out loud once. If it doesn't sound like them, it goes back. The rule: keep their words, their rhythm and their opinions, and cut anything a different founder could have said.

The founders, companies and customer quotes above are fictional and written only to demonstrate voice matching.
